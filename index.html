Who I Am

Economist, Author & Researcher

I am a Tanzanian economist, researcher and digital governance specialist with expertise in public policy, taxation, financial inclusion, media freedom and development research. I have experience working in programme management, policy analysis, academic research and digital journalism across local and international platforms.

My research concentrates on digital economy, taxation, mobile money, mobile fraud, regional trade integration, behavioural economics and economics of crime — sustained focus on East Africa. My newsroom career began in 2016 as a News Reporter at The Guardian Limited, Tanzania's leading English-language newspaper.

As a Fulbright Hubert H. Humphrey Fellow at Michigan State University (2024–25), I deepened expertise in data science, behavioural economics and international trade policy. I am now a Rotary Peace Fellow (2026–27), pursuing a PGD in Peace Building & Conflict Transformation at Makerere University, Uganda.

I am a Writing Fellow at African Liberty and an active peer reviewer for the African Tax Research Network (ATRN). I provide research, writing, training and consultancy services to organisations, researchers, media institutions and development partners.

Research & Writing

Publications & Writings

🔬 Ongoing Research Project
Beyond System Security: Addressing the Behavioral and Identity Gap in Mobile Money Fraud Prevention in Tanzania
This project examines the growing challenge of mobile money fraud in Tanzania by focusing on behavioural and identity-related gaps that existing security systems fail to address. Using FinScope Tanzania 2023 data, the research investigates how socio-demographic factors, behavioural patterns, digital usage methods and reliance on third-party SIM cards influence vulnerability to mobile money fraud. Through descriptive analysis and logistic regression, the study aims to generate evidence for a user-centered fraud prevention framework.
